The flossing debate has divided dentists for years. Traditional string floss has been the gold standard for decades, but water flossers have gained massive popularity. Which one should you actually use?

We dug into 15 clinical studies to give you a straight answer.

## The Short Answer

**Both work.** But they work differently:

- **String floss** is better at scraping plaque off tooth surfaces between teeth
- **Water flossers** are better at cleaning below the gumline and around dental work

The best choice depends on your specific dental situation. Here's the detailed breakdown.

## String Floss: The Classic

**Best for:** Healthy teeth with normal spacing, tight contacts between teeth

String floss physically scrapes the biofilm (plaque) off the sides of your teeth — surfaces your toothbrush can't reach. The mechanical scraping action is very effective at disrupting bacterial colonies.

### Pros
- Cheapest option (pennies per use)
- Most effective at removing interproximal plaque
- No charging or maintenance required
- Portable — fits in your pocket
- Dentist-recommended for decades

### Cons
- Difficult technique — most people do it wrong
- Hard to reach back molars
- Can cause gum bleeding if done incorrectly
- People with braces, bridges, or implants struggle with it
- Compliance is low — only 30% of people floss daily

### Proper Technique (Most People Get This Wrong)
1. Use 18 inches of floss, wind around middle fingers
2. Hold 1-2 inches between thumbs and index fingers
3. Slide gently between teeth — don't snap
4. **Curve into a C-shape** against one tooth and scrape up/down
5. Repeat for the other side of the gap
6. Use a clean section of floss for each gap

The C-shape curve is the step most people skip. Going straight up and down misses most of the plaque.

## Water Flosser: The Modern Alternative

**Best for:** Braces, implants, bridges, gum disease, arthritis, anyone who won't floss otherwise

A water flosser uses a pressurized stream of water to blast food particles and bacteria from between teeth and below the gumline. It's less about scraping plaque and more about flushing debris.

### Pros
- Much easier to use than string floss
- Superior at cleaning below the gumline (gum pockets)
- Excellent for braces, implants, and dental work
- Reduces gingivitis more effectively than string floss (per several studies)
- Better for people with dexterity issues
- People actually use it consistently

### Cons
- Costs $30-$80 upfront
- Needs counter space and an outlet (countertop models)
- Messy until you get the technique down
- Less effective at scraping tight interproximal plaque
- Requires water refills during use

### Key Research

A 2013 study in the *Journal of Clinical Dentistry* found that water flossers were **29% more effective** than string floss at removing plaque overall, and **51% more effective** at reducing gingivitis.

However, a separate study found string floss was better at removing plaque from tight contact points between teeth specifically.

## Who Should Use What

**Use string floss if:**
- You have healthy teeth with normal spacing
- You have good dexterity
- You've mastered the C-shape technique
- You're on a tight budget

**Use a water flosser if:**
- You have braces, implants, bridges, or crowns
- You have gum disease or deep gum pockets
- You find string floss difficult or painful
- You have arthritis or limited hand mobility
- You want the easiest possible routine

**Use BOTH if:**
- You want the absolute best results
- String floss for plaque scraping + water flosser for gum irrigation
- This is what most periodontists recommend for patients with gum disease

## The Bottom Line

The best flossing method is **the one you'll actually do every day**. A water flosser used daily beats string floss used once a week.

If you're currently not flossing at all, start with a water flosser — compliance rates are dramatically higher, and the gum health benefits are well-documented.

If you're disciplined with string floss and have healthy teeth, there's no need to switch. But if you have any dental work or gum issues, adding a water flosser to your routine is one of the best investments you can make.
